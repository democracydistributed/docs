# What is Democracy Distributed?

## The idea

If someone searches for their city council, a school board, or a community group, they should land on one page that shows how that organization actually runs:

- **its money:** where it comes from and where it goes
- **its decisions:** what was proposed, debated, and voted on
- **its people:** who holds which role
- **its projects:** what the money and decisions produced

Democracy Distributed (DD) builds that page, using one standard data model that works for any organization, anywhere.

## The problem

People who care about their community spend a lot of time trying to understand how decisions get made and how money gets spent. The information usually exists, but it's scattered:

- Decisions sit in meeting agendas and minutes.
- Money sits in budgets, financial statements, and contract databases.
- Outcomes sit in project pages and news stories, or aren't recorded at all.

Because nothing connects these, it's hard to participate, hard to hold anyone accountable, and hard to build trust. **DD's job is to connect them.**

## Three products, one data model

**WikiGov** is the public transparency layer. It's a Wikipedia-style record of governments: their finances, decisions, structure, and projects. It's free to read and open to contribute. → [WikiGov](wikigov.md)

**POD (People Organized Digitally)** turns the same structure into a tool for community groups (tenant associations, co-ops, neighbourhood groups) so they can decide, spend, and report transparently. *POD is at the vision stage.*

**The Contributor Network** (working name) is the engine under both. It's the people who find, link, verify, and add what official sources leave out, plus the tools, review, and credit that make their work count. → [The Contributor Network](contributor-network.md)

WikiGov and POD use the same building blocks: organizations, decisions, projects, and transactions. A tenant group and a city council are recorded the same way, which is what makes comparison and transparency possible at every scale.

## What makes DD different

Other civic projects do one part of this well. DD connects the parts.

| Others show… | DD adds… |
|---|---|
| Government finances | The decisions that authorized the spending |
| Council decisions | The projects and money those decisions set in motion |
| Contracts and bids | The approvals and budgets behind them |

The connections between these datasets usually aren't published anywhere. DD builds them from public sources, and **contributors fill the gaps**. That's why the Contributor Network is central to DD, not an add-on.

## Principles

- **Honest data.** Every number has a source. When a link is missing, DD shows the gap instead of guessing.
- **Open.** Free to read, open to contribute, with a public edit history.
- **Non-partisan.** DD records what happened. It doesn't campaign.
