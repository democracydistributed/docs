# Start here: Democracy Distributed in 2 minutes

## The question we answer

When a city council votes, money moves, and something gets built (or doesn't). These three facts live in different places:

- **Decisions** sit in council agendas and minutes.
- **Money** sits in budgets, financial statements, and contract records.
- **Outcomes** sit in project pages, news, or nowhere at all.

No public source connects them. **DD does.** Our core question:

> **Can a resident trace money → decision → outcome?**

## The chain of accountability

```
 Council decision (Item)  →  Project  →  Contract / Procurement  →  Payment (Transaction)  →  Outcome
```

Every link in that chain is a record on DD, with a source. Where a link is missing, DD says so openly instead of guessing.

## What's live today

On [democracydistributed.com](https://democracydistributed.com), the **City of Toronto** page has:

- **Finances:** an interactive Sankey chart showing where the money comes from and where it goes
- **Items:** council decisions, linked to the projects they authorized
- **Projects** and **People:** who holds which role, and what's being built
- **Trace panel:** click any flow to see the chain behind it

Hundreds of Toronto council decisions are already linked to the projects they authorized.

## Two products, one data model

- **WikiGov** is the public transparency layer: governments, open to everyone, built up by contributors.
- **POD** (People Organized Digitally) is the same structure, turned into a tool for community groups to decide and spend together. Tenant groups, co-ops and neighbourhood associations would get the same transparency we ask of governments.
