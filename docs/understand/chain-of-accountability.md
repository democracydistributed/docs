# The chain of accountability

Everything on DD comes back to one question:

> **Can a resident trace money → decision → outcome?**

## The chain

```
Suggestion → Item → Vote → Project → Transaction → Ledger
 (idea)     (proposal) (decision) (the work) (the payment) (the accounts)
```

| Link | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Suggestion** | Public input. Anyone can raise one, and several similar suggestions can feed one Item. | "The path in the park is falling apart." |
| **Item** | A formal proposal that needs a meeting and a decision. One project may need several Items, e.g. one to approve the work and one to award the contract. | "Authorize a $50,000 tender to resurface the park path." |
| **Vote** | The decision itself: when, and the result. | Passed, 8–1. |
| **Project** | The approved initiative, with scope and budget. It's optional: some decisions move money directly. | "Park path resurfacing." |
| **Transaction** | The money moving: who paid whom, how much, and when. It links back to the Project or Item that authorized it. | $15,000 paid to the paving contractor. |
| **Ledger** | The accounting entries. Every Transaction creates a debit and a credit. | Parks budget −$15,000. |

## Three ways money moves

Not every payment follows the full chain.

1. **Paid at a milestone.** A task within a project is completed, then paid. This is typical for contracts paid in stages.
2. **Paid when the project is done.** A simple project with one deliverable.
3. **Paid directly on the vote.** No project at all, as with grants, transfers, and releases of earmarked funds. The vote *is* the trigger.

## Not every payment needs a vote

Organizations delegate routine spending. On DD:

- **Hiring and contracts** always need a decision (Item + Vote).
- **Larger purchases** need a decision above the organization's threshold.
- **Routine operational spending** is delegated to a role. DD records who made it and in what position.

## Gaps are shown, not hidden

In the real world, most chains are incomplete. Official sources usually publish the **decision** at one end and the **reported outcome** at the other. The steps in between are rarely published anywhere.

DD shows exactly which links are missing. Each missing link is a specific, doable task for a contributor. Even half a chain is useful, because it shows where the gap is.

DD records **documented facts and gaps**. It never asserts wrongdoing.
