# WikiGov

WikiGov is DD's public transparency layer: a Wikipedia-style record of government finances, decisions, and structure. It's free to read and open to contribute.

**Status:** live for the City of Toronto at [democracydistributed.com](https://democracydistributed.com).

## The organization page

Every organization gets one page, findable by searching its name. It has four tabs:

| Tab | Shows |
|---|---|
| **Finances** | An interactive Sankey chart: revenue on the left, spending on the right, with each flow sized by dollars. |
| **Items** | The organization's decisions: motions, reports, and approvals. |
| **Projects** | What's being built or delivered, and its status. |
| **People** | Who holds which role, including roles where the holder isn't yet recorded. |

## Follow the money

The Finances tab is where most people start:

1. Click a spending category.
2. See the payments inside it.
3. Click a payment to open the **trace panel**, which shows the chain behind it: the decision that authorized it, the vote, and the project it paid for.

Where part of the chain is missing, the trace panel says so, and that gap is something a contributor can fill.

## Planned vs. actual

Where an organization publishes a budget, planned amounts appear as greyed flows. As real spending comes in, the flows fill with colour. This shows at a glance how much of what was promised has happened.

## Where the data comes from

WikiGov starts from official open data and builds on work by other civic projects, including:

- City financial statements and open budget data
- Council decisions from the City's meeting records
- Procurement and contract records
- Related civic projects such as Canada Spends and Civic Dashboard

Every figure links back to its source.
